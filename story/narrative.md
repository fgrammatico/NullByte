# NULLBYTE: The Hiring Protocol

## Background

HEXCORE is an offensive security firm that recruits through a controlled technical evaluation. Candidates enter a simulated corporate network and attempt to gain root access before its defenses shut them out.

The host runs NullByte in a local Minecraft Bedrock world. One or more players can join the same session. Discoveries, permission, noise, defenses, gates, and victory are shared. Leaving, dying, or reconnecting does not reset completed investigation work.

Minecraft chat is the portable HEXCORE terminal. Physical rooms, redstone systems, NPC conversations, and terminal operations form one connected scenario.

## Characters

### ZERO

HEXCORE's CISO and the architect of the evaluation. ZERO communicates through terse signs and terminal messages.

### GHOST

A former HEXCORE developer who left evidence throughout the facility. Their motives remain unclear.

### SENTINEL

HEXCORE's automated defense system. It reacts to the shared noise level with patrols, terminal restrictions, and access revocation.

### DR4K3

A compromised system administrator in the restricted-services segment. Careful evidence gathering is required before DR4K3 will trust an operator.

## The World

### Overworld: eth0

The exposed corporate network contains offices, server infrastructure, security operations, and hardware labs. Players begin here and establish their first foothold.

### Nether: eth1

The internal-services segment is protected by active defenses. Players must preserve access while investigating routes deeper into the network.

### The End: eth2

The isolated System Core contains the final technical gates. Root access is possible only after all required evidence and system conditions are satisfied.

## Shared Session

The world has no formal team object or private progression. Physical changes and discoveries apply to the whole session. Defensive events may revoke active permission, but completed puzzle discoveries remain recorded.

Individual scoring is deferred and does not control progression.

## Tone

NullByte uses dry technical writing. Clues reward observation and correlation. Wrong answers have consequences, but each challenge provides enough in-world evidence to solve it without guessing.
